ABSTRACT

Religious pilgrimage centers and monumental temple complexes represent some of the highest-density human gatherings on Earth. Leading Indian temples such as Tirupati, Vaishno Devi, Puri Jagannath, and Sabarimala routinely register daily congregations exceeding 80,000 to 150,000 devotees. Conventional crowd control mechanisms at these sites remain fundamentally physical, relying on miles of serpentine steel barricades, holding compartments, and manual baton policing. Within these static holding zones, devotees are subjected to 6 to 12 hours of agonizing standing time under extreme heat and humidity with zero awareness of queue velocity or expected Sanctum arrival times.

This opacity triggers acute psychological stress, medical emergencies, queue-jumping disputes, and catastrophic crowd crush risks when bottlenecks occur at the Sanctum threshold. In response to this crisis, this project presents DarshanQ, an intelligent, full-stack, real-time queue orchestration and crowd density management platform.

Key Architectural Innovations

  • Virtual Tokenization: Replaces physical queue lines with cryptographic virtual tokens, allowing devotees to wait in open courtyards, community halls, or local hotels.
  • Directed Graph Temple Topology: Mathematically models temple pathways as a directed graph consisting of 3 Entry Gates feeding into 3 Security Checkpoints, converging into a single Holy Inner Sanctum.
  • Explainable Queue Intelligence Engine: Implements a deterministic, explainable wait-time formula driven by a 15-minute sliding scan window velocity ($W = N_{ahead} / \max(R_{scan}, 0.1)$), replacing opaque ML predictions.
  • Bi-Directional WebSocket Sync: Uses Socket.IO room multiplexing to broadcast live queue progression, density updates, and proximity alerts instantaneously with zero browser refreshes.
  • Omni-Channel Telegram Bot: Provides headless smartphone tracking via Telegram, allowing devotees to receive automated turn alerts even with closed web applications.
Core Engineering Philosophy: Transforming mass crowd management from reactive containment to proactive, transparent, and algorithmic flow control.

Managing this scale of human movement presents immense operational challenges. Historically, temple administrations have managed surges using physical crowd control techniques developed decades ago: static zig-zag metal barricades, compartmentalized holding cages, and manual physical cordons.

1.2 Problem Definition: The Physical Queue Crisis

The traditional paradigm of temple crowd management suffers from four systemic structural vulnerabilities:

1. The "Black Box" Queue & Zero Velocity Visibility

Devotees entering the queue have zero information regarding actual waiting times, clearance velocity, or internal delays. Administrative signboards, when present, display static estimates that fail to reflect real-time bottlenecks. This psychological vacuum breeds anxiety, frustration, and unrest among pilgrims who have traveled long distances with families.

2. Physical Exhaustion & Vulnerable Demographic Distress

Pilgrims comprise elderly citizens, small children, pregnant women, and physically disabled devotees. Forcing these vulnerable individuals to stand upright for 6 to 12 hours in unventilated, crowded metal barricades leads to acute dehydration, heat exhaustion, fainting spells, and hypertensive crises. Emergency medical responders face severe difficulties accessing patients trapped deep within locked barricades.

3. Dangerous Crush Dynamics & Stampede Vulnerabilities

When holding compartments are opened, or when sudden bottlenecks occur near the sanctum sanctorum, differential pressure waves propagate through the crowd. Devotees at the rear, unaware of downstream obstructions, push forward, inducing crowd collapses and asphyxiation risks. Tragic historical stampedes across major pilgrimage shrines underscore the fatal dangers of relying exclusively on physical barriers.

CHAPTER 2: RELATED WORK

2.1 Literature Review on Queueing Systems & Crowd Dynamics

The mathematical modeling of waiting lines originated in the early 20th century with A.K. Erlang's pioneering work on telecommunication traffic, later generalized into classical Queueing Theory ($M/M/1$, $M/M/c$ models). In 1961, John Little formulated Little's Law:

$$L = \lambda W$$ Where $L$ is the average number of entities in the system, $\lambda$ is the long-term average arrival rate, and $W$ is the average time an entity spends in the system.

While Little's Law provides steady-state equilibrium bounds, human pilgrimage crowds violate Poisson arrival assumptions due to bursty, correlated arrivals during festival peaks. Furthermore, Helbing et al. (2000) demonstrated that human crowds exhibit fluid-like "turbulent flow" under extreme density (>5 persons/$m^2$), transitioning from smooth laminar flow to shockwaves that precipitate catastrophic stampedes. Effective crowd mitigation requires active throttling of ingress rates to preserve sub-critical density states.

Analysis of Existing Solutions

1. Tirumala Tirupati Devasthanams (TTD) Slotted Darshan

TTD pioneered online slotted ticket bookings and physical "SSD" (Sarva Darshan) counter tokens. However, the system requires pilgrims to pre-book months in advance or stand in physical counter queues at 3 AM to obtain wristbands. Crucially, once inside the temple complex, devotees are still routed into holding compartments for 4 to 8 hours with zero real-time queue velocity tracking.

2. Theme Park Virtual Systems (Disney Genie+ / FastPass)

Commercial leisure systems (Disney, Universal) utilize proprietary smartphone applications to assign virtual return windows. While highly effective, these systems are optimized for commercial upselling and individual ride kiosks, lacking the multi-lane convergence topologies and high-throughput bulk scanning essential for religious mass pilgrimages.

3. General Hospital & Banking Queuing Apps (QLess, Waitwhile)

Commercial SMS queue management platforms excel at single-station service desks (e.g., doctor consultations, bank tellers). However, they cannot model multi-stage spatial topologies where pilgrims must pass sequential security checks, luggage screening, and sanctum checkpoints with interconnected flow rates.

4.2 Directed Graph Temple Topology & Queue Intelligence

Physical temple layout is modeled as a directed acyclic graph $G = (V, E)$, ensuring deterministic forward line-of-progression routing.

LEVEL 1 (Entry Gates): [ Gate 1 ] [ Gate 2 ] [ Gate 3 ] │ │ │ ▼ ▼ ▼ LEVEL 2 (Security Checkpoints): [ Checkpoint 1 ] [ Checkpoint 2 ] [ Checkpoint 3 ] │ │ │ └────────────────┬─┴──────────────────┘ ▼ LEVEL 3 (Sanctum Sanctorum): [ Sanctum ] (Final Completion)

Topological Progression Invariants:

  • Gate Ingress: Devotee checks in at Gate $k$ ($k \in \{1, 2, 3\}$) ➔ token transitions from waiting to checked_in, advancing to Checkpoint $k$.
  • Security Clearance: Scanning at Checkpoint $k$ advances the token along $e = (\text{CP}_k, \text{Sanctum})$, converging into the Sanctum queue.
  • Final Darshan: Scanning at Sanctum completes the spiritual journey, marking token status = 'completed'.

Mathematical Formulation of the Queue Intelligence Engine

Unlike opaque machine learning models that produce erratic wait-time fluctuations, DarshanQ employs an explainable, deterministic formulation rooted in empirical queue velocity:

1. Rolling Empirical Scan Rate ($R_{scan}$): $$R_{scan}(c) = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{M} \mathbb{I}(\text{scanned\_at} \ge T_{now} - 15\text{ min})}{15}$$ Where $M$ is the count of scans logged in `checkpoint_logs` for station $c$ in the trailing 15-minute sliding window.
2. Estimated Devotee Wait Time ($W$): $$W(\tau) = \max\left(0, \; \left\lceil \frac{N_{ahead}(\tau)}{\max(R_{scan}(c), \; 0.1)} \right\rceil \right)$$ Where $N_{ahead}$ is the count of active tokens stationed at $c$ with token\_number < $\tau$.token\_number. Division by zero is safeguarded by clamping $\min(R_{scan}) = 0.1$.
3. Station Density Level ($D$): $$D(c) = \begin{cases} \text{LOW (Green)}, & \text{if } N_{waiting}(c) < 4 \\ \text{MODERATE (Amber)}, & \text{if } 4 \le N_{waiting}(c) < 8 \\ \text{HIGH (Red)}, & \text{if } N_{waiting}(c) \ge 8 \end{cases}$$

5.3 Cryptographic Authentication & RBAC Middleware

Security is maintained via stateless JSON Web Tokens (JWT) signed with HMAC-SHA256 and salted password hashing via bcryptjs (cost factor 12).

const jwt = require('jsonwebtoken');

function authenticate(req, res, next) {
  const header = req.headers['authorization'];
  if (!header || !header.startsWith('Bearer ')) {
    return res.status(401).json({ error: 'Missing or invalid Authorization header' });
  }
  const token = header.slice(7);
  try {
    const payload = jwt.verify(token, process.env.JWT_SECRET);
    req.user = payload; // Decoded payload: { id, role, name }
    next();
  } catch (err) {
    return res.status(401).json({ error: 'Token invalid or expired' });
  }
}

function requireRole(...roles) {
  return (req, res, next) => {
    if (!req.user) return res.status(401).json({ error: 'Not authenticated' });
    if (!roles.includes(req.user.role)) {
      return res.status(403).json({ 
        error: `Access denied. Required role: ${roles.join(' or ')}` 
      });
    }
    next();
  };
}

module.exports = { authenticate, requireRole };

const pool = require('../db/pool');
const SCAN_WINDOW_MINUTES = 15;
const DEFAULT_SCAN_RATE = 0.5; // Fallback: 0.5 scans/min (2 mins per devotee)

async function getScanRate(checkpointId) {
  try {
    const res = await pool.query(
      `SELECT COUNT(*)::int AS recent_scans FROM checkpoint_logs 
       WHERE checkpoint_id = $1 AND scanned_at >= NOW() - ($2 || ' minutes')::interval`,
      [checkpointId, SCAN_WINDOW_MINUTES]
    );
    const recentScans = res.rows[0]?.recent_scans || 0;
    const rate = recentScans / SCAN_WINDOW_MINUTES;
    return rate > 0 ? parseFloat(rate.toFixed(2)) : DEFAULT_SCAN_RATE;
  } catch (err) {
    return DEFAULT_SCAN_RATE;
  }
}

async function estimateWaitTime(tokenId) {
  try {
    const tokenRes = await pool.query(
      `SELECT id, token_number, temple_id, status, current_checkpoint_id 
       FROM queue_tokens WHERE id = $1`, [tokenId]
    );
    if (tokenRes.rows.length === 0) return { estimated_wait_minutes: 0, tokens_ahead: 0 };
    const token = tokenRes.rows[0];

    if (['completed', 'cancelled'].includes(token.status)) {
      return { estimated_wait_minutes: 0, tokens_ahead: 0, scan_rate: DEFAULT_SCAN_RATE };
    }

    // Count waiting tokens ahead in sequence order for the same station
    const aheadRes = await pool.query(
      `SELECT COUNT(*)::int AS tokens_ahead FROM queue_tokens 
       WHERE temple_id = $1 AND status IN ('waiting', 'notified', 'checked_in')
         AND (current_checkpoint_id = $2 OR current_checkpoint_id IS NULL)
         AND token_number < $3`,
      [token.temple_id, token.current_checkpoint_id, token.token_number]
    );
    const tokens_ahead = aheadRes.rows[0]?.tokens_ahead || 0;
    const scan_rate = token.current_checkpoint_id 
      ? await getScanRate(token.current_checkpoint_id) : DEFAULT_SCAN_RATE;

    // Mathematical formula with divide-by-zero safeguard
    const estimated_wait_minutes = Math.max(0, Math.round(tokens_ahead / Math.max(scan_rate, 0.1)));

    await pool.query(`UPDATE queue_tokens SET estimated_wait_minutes = $1 WHERE id = $2`,
      [estimated_wait_minutes, tokenId]);

    return { estimated_wait_minutes, tokens_ahead, scan_rate, token_number: token.token_number };
  } catch (err) {
    return { estimated_wait_minutes: 0, tokens_ahead: 0, scan_rate: DEFAULT_SCAN_RATE };
  }
}

7.2 Known Simplifications & Architectural Trade-offs

In accordance with rigorous academic engineering standards, the prototype intentionally adopted specific architectural trade-offs:

1. Simulated Checkpoint Hardware vs. IoT Turnstiles

Physical production deployment requires optical RFID turnstiles or automated optical scanners. For this Full Stack Development prototype, checkpoint scans are driven through staff UI interactions (+1 Pass Next Devotee). This deliberate choice allowed complete focus on software distributed state management, WebSockets, and database concurrency without dependencies on external physical microcontrollers.

2. Explainable Heuristics vs. Black-Box Machine Learning

While deep neural networks (LSTM, Transformers) could theoretically model wait times, they suffer from fatal explainability deficits. When an ML model predicts 45 minutes and the devotee waits 90 minutes, administration cannot explain why. DarshanQ's velocity formula ($W = N_{ahead} / R_{scan}$) is mathematically verifiable and explainable, building devotee trust.

3. Un-slotted Arrivals vs. Hourly Reservation Windows

The current prototype operates on dynamic FIFO queuing with real-time ingress balancing. In future enterprise rollouts, hourly reservation quotas (e.g. 2,000 tokens/hour) will be layered onto the virtual token engine to prevent bunching during peak festival mornings.
